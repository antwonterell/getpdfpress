Why scanned PDFs behave differently

Digital PDFs can store text as objects; scans contain page images and may also have an OCR text layer. Recompressing images or rasterizing pages can affect both visual detail and selectability. Keep the original and evaluate the actual output.

A scan and a digital export of similar-looking pages can have very different sizes. Neither a page count nor the fact that a document contains digital text guarantees a 200KB result.

The readability sweet spot: DPI, color, quality

  • DPI: test resolution settings against the smallest writing and required marks. Lower resolution reduces pixels, but there is no universal readable threshold or fixed savings ratio. Follow any resolution requirement from the recipient.
  • Color: grayscale or black and white may reduce size, but can remove meaningful color or faint marks. Use them only when permitted, then compare the scan with the original.
  • JPEG quality: lower settings can introduce artifacts around letters and thin lines. The public tools choose settings internally; inspect the result rather than assuming a quality label proves readability.

A safe compression workflow

  1. Upload to the compressor and choose your portal's target.
  2. Open the 500KB or 200KB tool that matches your requirement. Upload the PDF and press Compress; there is no Gentle, Balanced, or Strong selector.
  3. Download the result and inspect small text, stamps, and signatures. The tool reports size, but you must assess readability yourself.
  4. If the result exceeds the limit or loses required detail, improve the source. Split only if separate uploads are allowed, and remove only pages that are not required.

Rescuing a scan that won't compress

Skewed or low-contrast source images need source improvements, not just more compression. Rescan in even light with the camera parallel to the page. Test settings on a copy and preserve all required edges, color and marks.

How to verify readability before submitting

Open the original and output side by side at 100% zoom, then zoom further to inspect faint writing, stamps and signatures. If details are lost, keep the clearer source and improve it or ask about accepted alternatives. Split only when separate files are permitted.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my scanned PDF barely compress?

It may already be optimized. Previously compressed scans, screenshot PDFs, and web captures often have little left to squeeze. The biggest gains come from high-DPI color scans and camera photos with large margins. Crop, convert unnecessary color to grayscale, then compress once and proofread the result.

What DPI should I scan documents at?

Follow the receiving service's resolution requirements. If none are stated, test more than one setting on a copy and inspect the smallest writing, stamps and signatures. DPI alone does not guarantee readability or a particular file size.

Is black-and-white scanning a good idea?

Sometimes. Pure black-and-white can make tiny files with crisp typed text, but it can erase light pencil, faded stamps, and subtle seals. Grayscale is the safer default for mixed documents. After scanning, compress toward your upload target and confirm every mark you need is still visible.

Can OCR reduce my scan file size?

OCR adds a text layer for searching. By itself it does not shrink the page images. Some tools re-encode images while running OCR, which can reduce size as a side effect, but you should treat OCR as a readability feature. For upload limits, crop, grayscale, and image compression matter more.

How do I keep a scan readable at 500KB or 200KB?

Keep every required page. Choose a target that fits the stated limit, then inspect the smallest writing and marks. If the result is unclear, improve the source or split only when separate uploads are allowed.

Test the source before retrying

Keep one original scan. Change one scan setting at a time on a copy, compare the faintest marks and any required color, then compare exact bytes. Do not treat a lower DPI or grayscale setting as proof of readability. For text selection and PDF-level checks, see the output-quality checklist.
