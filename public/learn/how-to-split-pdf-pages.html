How to Split a PDF Into Separate Pages

By the getPDFpress team · Updated July 21, 2026

Fast answer: Upload your PDF to the Split tool and every page comes back as its own PDF, packaged in a ZIP. Keep the pages you need, and recombine any subset with the Merge tool.

What splitting solves

Splitting is the answer to "I only need part of this." One signed page from a fifty-page contract. The first page of a bank statement, without four pages of transactions a landlord does not need. A double-sided scan where someone else's document ended up in your file. A single chapter to share instead of the entire manual. In each case, sending the whole file is oversharing, oversized, or both.

Splitting is also the first half of page-level editing. Split a document into pages, discard some, reorder others, merge the survivors — together with the Merge tool you can restructure any PDF without desktop software.

Step by step

  1. Open the Split tool and upload your PDF. Single-page files are rejected with a clear message, since there is nothing to split.
  2. Run the split. Every page is extracted into its own standalone PDF, numbered in order — page-1.pdf, page-2.pdf, and so on.
  3. Download the ZIP containing all the pages.
  4. Unpack it and keep the pages you need. Each one is a complete, valid PDF ready to send, upload, or merge.

Working with the ZIP file

Pages arrive in a ZIP because delivering fifty separate downloads would be miserable. Opening it is built into every platform: double-click on Windows or macOS to browse the pages inside; on iPhone, tapping the ZIP in the Files app unpacks it; on Android, the Files-by-Google app or any file manager extracts it. Pull out the pages you need and delete the rest — they are all reproducible by splitting again.

The page numbering matches the original document's order, so page-7.pdf is the seventh page of your source file. For long documents, opening the original side by side and noting the page numbers you need before unpacking saves hunting.

Rebuilding a smaller, better document

The split-then-merge pattern handles a family of real problems:

Fitting upload limits. When compression alone cannot get a scan under a strict limit readably, dropping pages is the honest lever — a form that needs pages 1, 3, and 9 does not need the other seventeen. Split, select, merge, then compress the small result. This pipeline appears throughout our too-large guide because it works.

Fixing scan accidents. Blank pages, upside-down inserts, and stray pages from the scanner's feeder disappear by rebuilding without them.

Reordering. Pages scanned out of sequence merge back in the right order — select them correctly during the merge and the document is fixed.

Sharing selectively. Extract exactly what each recipient needs. The signed page to the counterparty; the full contract to your records. Less oversharing, smaller attachments, faster sends.

Frequently asked questions

Can I split just a page range instead of every page?

The tool splits everything into single pages; you then keep the range you need from the ZIP and merge those pages back into one file. The result is identical to range extraction, with one extra step.

Does splitting reduce quality?

No. Pages are copied exactly as they exist in the original — same resolution, same content, no recompression.

Why is one extracted page almost as big as my whole document?

Resources like fonts are shared across a document; each extracted page must carry its own copies. Compressing the pages you keep reclaims most of the overhead.

How many pages can the tool handle?

Documents up to 25MB split fine. Very long documents produce very full ZIPs, so on phones with limited storage, extract selectively rather than unpacking hundreds of pages.
